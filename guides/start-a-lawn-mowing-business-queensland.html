How to start a lawn mowing business in Queensland

A plain guide for the first weeks and months: what to register, what to insure, which work needs a licence, when you can mow, and the paperwork to set up from day one.

Checked against the official pages on 15 November 2026. General information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Rules and fees change, so follow the links to the official pages before you act.

The short version

  • Get an ABN. It costs nothing.
  • Register a business name with ASIC, unless you trade under your own first name and surname.
  • Work out whether you need to register for GST, and keep an eye on it every month.
  • Talk to an insurance broker about public liability insurance before the first job.
  • If you take anyone on, get workers' compensation insurance first.
  • Check the licence before you offer weed spraying, pest control or landscaping with structures.
  • Mow inside the hours the noise rules allow.
  • Keep every quote, invoice and receipt from day one.

Register the business

Start with an ABN (Australian business number). It's free to register for an ABN through the Australian Business Register.

The Australian Business Register says you're entitled to an ABN if you're carrying on or starting an enterprise in Australia, and that you're not entitled to one for work you do as an employee.

Next, the name. According to ASIC, you must register a business name if you run a business in Australia and are not trading under your own name. A sole trader using their first name and surname, and nothing else, does not need to: John Smith would not register 'John Smith', but would need to register 'John Smith Gardening'. ASIC's fee to register a business name is $47 for 1 year or $108 for 3 years.

Tax basics

GST is a tax of 10% on most goods, services and other items sold or consumed in Australia. You must register for GST if your business has a GST turnover of $75,000 or more. If you've started a new business, you should register if you expect your GST turnover to reach $75,000 in the first year. You have to register for GST within 21 days of becoming aware that your GST turnover will go over the threshold. GST turnover is your business income, not your profit. Below the threshold, registering is optional.

Income tax is yours to sort out too. The ABR says most people who have an ABN are required to lodge an annual income tax return, and that you are responsible for your own income tax, superannuation and GST (if applicable).

Put part of every payment into a separate account for tax, and for GST if you are registered, so the bill is not a surprise. An accountant can help you work out how much. If you register for GST, the BAS due dates page shows when each one is due.

Keep your records from the first job. business.gov.au says you need to keep most business records for 5 years, from when you got the records or completed the transactions they relate to, whichever is later.

Insurance

Public liability insurance covers injury to other people or damage to their property while you are on the job. Business Queensland suggests talking to a business insurance broker or business adviser to find what insurance you need.

If you take on staff, WorkCover Queensland says: if you employ workers in Queensland, you must have workers' compensation insurance. Ask WorkCover Queensland who counts as a worker before anyone starts, and ask your accountant about super and tax for staff.

Licences in Queensland

Mowing, edging and tidying gardens are not in the licensed work below. Some of the extras mowing businesses get asked for are. For your exact mix of services, check the Australian Business Licence and Information Service or ask the regulator before you quote.

Landscaping with structures

The QBCC says that in Queensland, individuals and companies must hold a QBCC licence to carry out building work valued over $3,300. The QBCC's structural landscaping licence class covers work such as carports, decking, fences, gazebos, pergolas, retaining walls, and paving or concrete not intended to carry vehicles. If a job heads that way, check with the QBCC first.

Weed spraying

Under Queensland's Agricultural Chemicals Distribution Control Act 1966, if you use ground equipment to spray herbicide on land that you or a close relative does not own or occupy, you may need a commercial operators licence. Whether you need one depends on the type of equipment and on where in Queensland the work is done. Business Queensland's page on the commercial operators licence explains it.

Equipment that is hand-powered is one of the exemptions outside hazardous areas. The business that directs licensed operators needs a ground distribution contractor's licence, as Business Queensland's licensing page sets out. Check with Business Queensland before you offer spraying.

Pest control

Queensland Health says a pest management activity using a pesticide or a fumigant must only be undertaken by a person who holds a current pest management licence. If a customer asks you to treat for insects around the house, check with Queensland Health first.

When you can mow

Under the Environmental Protection Act 1994 (section 440S), a regulated device must not be operated in a way that makes an audible noise on a business day or Saturday before 7.00am or after 7.00pm, or on any other day before 8.00am or after 7.00pm. Regulated devices include grass-cutters such as lawnmowers, ride-on mowers, edge cutters and string trimmers, and leaf-blowers.

An early start in summer is tempting. Plan the first job of the day for somewhere a mower will not wake anyone, or start with work that makes no noise. For anything else about noise where you work, check with the local council.

Green waste

Where you can take clippings and prunings, and what it costs, depends on the council area. Check with the council for each area you work in, using the Queensland Local Government Directory, and put tip runs into your prices.

Gear to start with

Buy what the first jobs need, not what a big crew runs. A starting kit for suburban yards is usually:

  • a mower that suits the yard sizes you will do
  • a line trimmer and an edger
  • a blower
  • a hedge trimmer, if you will offer hedges
  • fuel cans, a rake, tarps and green waste bags
  • hearing and eye protection, gloves, boots and sun protection
  • a ute or a trailer, with ramps and a way to tie everything down

Keep a list of each machine and when it was last serviced. A blunt blade and a dirty air filter cost you time on every job.

Pricing your first jobs

Work out what an hour of your time has to cover before you quote anyone. Count fuel, servicing, insurance, tip fees, the phone, and the time spent driving between jobs, not only the time on the mower. The hourly rate calculator turns your costs and the hours you can work into a rate.

Then price each yard from that rate: how long it takes you, plus the drive and the tip. Write the price down in a quote so you and the customer agree on what is included. For regular customers, agree how often you will come, such as every 2 weeks or every 4 weeks, and what happens in the slow months.

Getting the first customers

  • Start close to home. Neighbours, family and friends, then the streets around them. Jobs close together mean less driving.
  • Letterbox the streets you already mow. A neat yard next door is the best advertisement you have.
  • Post in local community groups. Say what you do, the suburbs you cover and how to reach you.
  • Answer quickly. Reply the same day, turn up when you said you would, and send the quote while the customer still remembers you.
  • Ask happy customers to tell a neighbour. Most new work comes from the streets you already mow.

Questions

Do I need a licence to mow lawns in Queensland?

Mowing, edging and tidying gardens are not in the licensed work this guide covers. Spraying herbicide on other people's land, pest control and landscaping with structures can need a licence. For your exact services, check the Australian Business Licence and Information Service or ask the regulator.

Do I have to register for GST when I start?

Not straight away, unless you expect your GST turnover to reach $75,000 in the first year. You must register for GST if your business has a GST turnover of $75,000 or more, within 21 days of becoming aware that you will go over the threshold. Below the threshold, registering is optional.

What time can I start mowing?

Under the Environmental Protection Act 1994, not before 7.00am on a business day or Saturday, and not before 8.00am on any other day. On every day, a mower must not be run in a way that makes an audible noise after 7.00pm.

Do I need a business name?

Only if you trade under something other than your own first name and surname. John Smith needs no business name to trade as 'John Smith', but 'John Smith Gardening' must be registered with ASIC.

How much should I charge?

Work out what an hour of your time has to cover, including driving, fuel, servicing, insurance and tip fees, then price each yard from that rate. The free hourly rate calculator on this site does the sums.
