Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 November 2026

This policy explains what personal information Kept collects, why, and what we do — and don't do — with it. It covers both www.keptsoftware.com.au (this website) and app.keptsoftware.com.au (the app itself).

The short version: Kept offers two storage modes, chosen per business in Settings → Account & sync. In Secure mode, the jobs, clients and invoices you enter are encrypted on your device before they ever reach our servers, using a key derived from your own login password — we physically cannot read that data, not to help with support, not if we wanted to. In Standard mode (the default), that same data is stored in ordinary, readable form on our servers instead — simpler, with nothing to lose if a password is forgotten. Either way we can see your business name, your login email, and which parts of the app get used. The one exception for a Secure-mode business is Account Recovery Assistance, an optional feature that provides a safety net against lockout, usable only with a private key that's kept completely offline and never touches our servers.

1. Who we are

Kept is operated by Lawn Envy Australia (ABN 19 863 392 800), a Brisbane-based business. The app began as software we built to run our own work and is now available to other Australian service and trade businesses. For anything in this policy, contact us via the form on this site or at the email address you've been given directly.

2. What we collect

When you sign up or use the app:

  • Your login email and password (the password itself is never stored anywhere — only a cryptographic check that a login attempt used the right one)
  • Your business name and phone/address if you provide them at setup — these are stored in plain text, since we need them to run the admin side of the service
  • Which features of the app you open (e.g. "Invoices", "Schedule") and when — used only to understand which parts of the product actually get used, never tied to what's inside those features

What you enter into the app — jobs, client names and contact details, quotes, invoices, expenses, staff records, documents, and the lawn sizes and outlines you choose to keep — is stored the way your business has chosen in Settings → Account & sync. In Secure mode it's encrypted on your device before it's sent anywhere, and we store only the resulting ciphertext — we cannot decrypt it, read it, search it, or hand it over in a readable form, including in response to a support request or a legal request for data we don't have the technical ability to produce in plain text, other than for businesses that have opted into Account Recovery Assistance (see above), where it's still only possible using a private key we hold entirely offline, never on any server. In Standard mode (the default), that same data is stored in ordinary, readable form, the way most business software works.

If you contact us — through the "get in touch" form on this site, by phone, or by email — we keep what you tell us (name, email, business name, phone if given, and the content of your message) so we can follow up.

When you sign up or leave your details, we save which link or ad brought you here (the campaign tags in the web address, the website you came from and the page you landed on) so we know which advertising works. We keep this in our own database and do not send it to anyone.

3. What we don't do

  • We don't use any third-party analytics, advertising trackers, or marketing pixels inside the app itself. This website does run the Meta (Facebook/Instagram) Pixel, so we can measure how our ads perform and show them to people who've visited before — it sees which pages are viewed and when someone taps Start free, tries the demo or sends the contact form, and never anything you enter into the app.
  • We don't sell, rent, or share your information with third parties for their own marketing purposes.
  • We don't read, scan, or process your business data for our own purposes; for a Secure-mode business, we technically can't.

4. Where your data is stored

Our primary database and login system run on Supabase, in Sydney, Australia. That is where your account details, your business contact details, your billing state and any marketplace job requests are held — so the bulk of the readable personal information we hold about you stays onshore.

For a Secure-mode business, everything entered into the app — jobs, clients, quotes, invoices — is encrypted on your device before it is sent, so what sits in that database is ciphertext we cannot decrypt. For a Standard-mode business (the default), that same data sits in the database in ordinary, readable form, alongside your account details.

Optional map features. The maps in the app, and Measure lawn, send map information to a mapping company, and only after you say yes inside the app; each can be turned off again in Settings → Appearance. The maps (Today, a day's map in Jobs, the Clients map and the Round planner's map) ask OpenStreetMap for the map pictures and, on Today and a day's map, to find where each job is. Measure lawn, for lawn care and landscaping businesses, shows a satellite picture of a lawn so you can trace it and get its size: Kept asks Esri, a mapping company in the United States, for the pictures of the area you are looking at. Esri sees that area (close up, the property and its surroundings) and your device's IP address. It is never sent a name, a phone number or anything about the job. To find where an address is, Kept uses OpenStreetMap's free address search, the same one the Today map uses. These requests go out in readable form, including for a Secure-mode business, because the picture has to be fetched and the address looked up. If you choose to keep a lawn's size, it is saved with that client like the rest of your business data (encrypted on your device first, for a Secure-mode business). The size is an estimate from a picture that can be a few years old.

Notifications. If the owner of a business, or a member of its staff, turns on notifications on a device (Settings → Account & sync → Notifications), we keep one record for that device in our Sydney database: the address that Apple's, Google's, Mozilla's or Microsoft's notification service gave the device, two keys that go with it, which kinds of notification you asked for, a rough label for the device ("iPhone or iPad", "Android" or "Computer"), and, for a member of staff, a short code that says which person in the business's staff list they are (a code, not a name). We use it only to send you what you asked for, and we delete it when you turn notifications off or the service tells us the device is gone. A notification is encrypted for your device before it leaves us, so the company that carries it can't read it: it sees that one was sent, when, and to which address. A notification about a quote a customer accepted or declined, or about a new marketplace request, is made from information we already hold in readable form: the quote's number and amount, or the type of work and the suburb. The two optional evening notes, tomorrow's jobs and things that need attention, are different: your device works them out from your own business data and sends them to us as a short readable message, even for a Secure-mode business, because we cannot read that data and the note has to be delivered at about 5pm Brisbane time. It holds a count and a time, such as "Tomorrow: 4 jobs. The first starts at 7:30am." or "2 invoices overdue". We keep it only until it is sent, and delete anything unsent after a week. Once it is sent we keep only a blank record that one went out that day, for up to a week, so the same note is never sent twice. A notification that a job has been given to a member of staff is made the same way: when someone in the business gives a staff member a job, the device of the person who gave it works out how many jobs and the day and time of the first, and sends us that with the staff member's code, even for a Secure-mode business, because we cannot read the jobs. It holds a count, a day and a time, such as "New job for you. Tue 10 Nov, 7:30am." We use it to send that one notification straight away and do not keep it. No notification ever carries a client's name, phone number or address, or what a customer wrote. Anyone who can see your lock screen can read a notification, so if you would rather it said nothing there, use your device's own setting (Notifications → Show Previews).

Overseas disclosure. Australian Privacy Principle 8 requires us to tell you if your personal information may go overseas and, where practicable, to which countries. Some of the providers on that page operate outside Australia — Stripe and Google process information in the United States, Xero in New Zealand and Australia, Open-Meteo in Germany, OpenStreetMap, if you turn on a map or Measure lawn, in the United Kingdom, Esri, if you turn on Measure lawn, in the United States, and Apple, Google, Mozilla and Microsoft, if you turn on notifications, in the United States and other countries where they operate. Vercel serves the site from whichever of its data centres is nearest you. Where the information involved is a Secure-mode business's data, an overseas provider holds only unreadable ciphertext. Information that is not encrypted — your login email, business contact details, anything you send us through the enquiry form, and, for a Standard-mode business (the default), its business data too — may be handled overseas in readable form. By using this site and the app you consent to that disclosure. We choose providers we consider reputable and rely on the privacy commitments each of them publishes.

If you connect your own email or SMS sending accounts (a Gmail app password, a ClickSend account) to send invoices or quotes, those credentials and the messages sent through them are handled by your own account with that provider, under their terms — not ours.

5. Other businesses using the app

Every business's data is fully isolated from every other business using the app, enforced at the database level regardless of storage mode — and, for Secure-mode businesses, reinforced by the encryption described above. No business using Kept can see another business's jobs, clients, invoices or numbers.

6. The request-a-quote marketplace

If you submit a job request through our marketplace, we collect what you tell us — the type of work, your suburb and postcode, a description, and your contact details. Businesses registered in that trade can see the type of work, your suburb and postcode and the description, so they can decide whether to quote; they do not see your contact details. Your name, address, phone number and email go to one business only, the one whose quote you accept. That is the entire purpose of collecting them, and they are stored in Sydney with everything else.

We do not sell that information, and we do not share it outside matching your job to businesses that can do it. Every job-lead email we send to a business carries an unsubscribe link, and you can ask us to withdraw a request or delete your details at any time.

Full details, including what we can and cannot undo once a request has gone out, are in the Marketplace Terms.

7. How long we keep data

  • Live demo accounts (created via the "try the demo" link) are automatically and permanently deleted 24 hours after creation.
  • Real business accounts are kept for as long as the account exists. If a business account is deleted, its data is permanently removed and cannot be recovered.
  • "Get in touch" enquiries are kept so we can follow up, and can be deleted on request.
  • Marketplace job requests are kept while the request is live and for a reasonable period afterwards, so the businesses quoting can follow up and so we can deal with any complaint about it. They are deleted after that, or sooner on request.

8. Your rights

You can ask us to tell you what plain-text information we hold about your business (login email, business name, contact details, feature usage), correct it, or delete your account entirely. For a Secure-mode business, because the content you enter into the app is encrypted with a key only you hold, we cannot selectively access or redact individual pieces of it on request — account deletion removes everything either way. For a Standard-mode business, that content is stored in readable form, so we can access, correct or delete specific records on request the same as any other data we hold.

If you're in Australia, you also have rights under the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles. If you have a concern we haven't resolved, you can contact the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au).

9. If something goes wrong

If a data breach occurs that's likely to cause you serious harm, we'll notify you and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner as required by the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. For a Secure-mode business, because your business data is encrypted on your device with a key we don't hold, a breach of our servers would expose ciphertext rather than readable client, job or invoice records — which substantially limits the harm such a breach could cause. For a Standard-mode business (the default), business data would be exposed in readable form, the same as any other unencrypted information we hold — login emails, business contact details and enquiries sent through this site — and would be assessed case by case.

10. Changes to this policy

If we make a material change to how we handle personal information, we'll update the date at the top of this page. Continued use of the app after a change means you accept the updated policy.
